# Cut-by-cut guide

Temperatures are °F with °C in brackets. Times are rough planning numbers. Weather, cooker,
wrapping and the individual piece of meat can swing them by hours, so **plan to finish 1–2 h
early and hold**. The pull temperature is a guide; for barbecue cuts, **tenderness decides**.

## Food safety floor (USDA)

- Whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb and veal (steaks, chops, roasts): **145°F (63°C)**, plus a
  3-minute rest.
- Ground meat and fresh sausage: **160°F (71°C)**.
- All poultry, including ground poultry: **165°F (74°C)**.
- Fish: **145°F (63°C)**.
- Holding cooked meat: keep it **at or above 140°F (60°C)**, or chill it.
- A common barbecue guideline is to get meat that has been injected, probed or ground from
  40°F to 140°F within about 4 hours. With whole intact muscle there's more margin, since
  bacteria sit on the surface, but a pit struggling to get the meat up is worth fixing
  urgently.

**Probe placement:** put the probe in the thickest part. For brisket that's the flat, since the
point is fattier and runs hotter. Stay away from fat seams and bone. Two probes in one brisket
is great: flat and point.

**Carryover** in low-and-slow meat is small, a few degrees, but a big hunk stays hot for hours
in a hold.

## Roasts and lean cuts (cook to a doneness, then rest; carryover matters)

Pull **5–10°F below** the final temperature for big roasts, because the rest carries them up.
Hot-and-fast cooks carry over more.

## Rest, hold and serve

- A long barbecue rest improves brisket and pork. Wrap it in foil or butcher paper, then a
  towel, in a dry cooler. It drops about 5–10°F per hour and holds safely above 140°F for
  roughly 3–4 h.
- An oven hold at 150–170°F works for many hours; check that the meat stays above 140°F.
- Slice brisket when it's about 150–165°F. Pull pork while it's hot, around 160–180°F; wear
  gloves.
- **Running late?** Raise the pit (brisket and pork butt handle 275–300°F fine), wrap through
  the stall, and use the hold time in reverse. **Running early?** Hold it. That's the easy
  direction.
